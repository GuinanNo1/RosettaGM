PatchWorker help

PatchWorker edits the synth patches of a Novation Circuit Tracks, with no hardware needed: the page plays each patch itself. It opens with the TracksGM pack, plays the projects in a pack so you can hear patches in context, and what you make goes out as .syx patches or a whole .circuittrackspack to load with Novation Components. Nothing is uploaded: files stay on your computer.

Since v0.9 PatchWorker works offline only: the browser is the synth, and a MIDI keyboard (a Circuit Tracks too) just plays it. Editing a Circuit Tracks directly, as Components does, may come back later as a separate mode.

Quick start

  1. Pick a patch from the list on the left.
  2. Play it: click the keys along the bottom, type on the A row of your keyboard (W E T Y U are the black keys, Z / X change octave), or press Space to run a loop while you edit. Keys S / M / L in the bottom bar sets the keyboard's size.
  3. Turn knobs: drag up or down. Hold Shift for fine steps, scroll over a knob, or use the arrow keys. Double-click a knob to reset it. Click the number under a knob to type a value.
  4. Download pack saves the whole pack with your edits, ready to send to the Circuit Tracks with Novation Components. Patch ▾ → Download .syx saves just the current patch.

Edits go straight into the pack in your browser, and your session is kept there, so closing the tab does not lose it. An orange dot marks every patch changed since the pack was opened. Undo (Ctrl/Cmd+Z) works per patch, Compare (hold it) plays the patch as the pack had it, and Patch ▾ → Revert puts it back (Undo brings your changes back again).

Saved, unsaved, and the Unsaved edits tray

The tag under the patch name says Saved or Edited · not saved. A patch is unsaved when you have changed it and not downloaded it since, as part of the pack (Download pack), a bank (Bank .syx) or its own .syx. While it is unsaved, Save .syx and Download pack sit beside the tag.

Every unsaved patch is listed under Unsaved edits on the left (the number is how many), newest first, with where it came from and when you changed it. Each has Open, .syx (download just that patch) and Discard (put it back as it was last saved; open it and press Undo to bring your edit back). Save all as .syx bank downloads them all in one file, which you can open here again or load with Components.

Saving is your decision. When you choose another patch while the one you are on has unsaved changes, PatchWorker asks once: Save .syx, Download pack, Not now (it stays in Unsaved edits) or Stay on this patch. It doesn't ask again about the same patch until you change it again. Tick Don't ask again to stop the question; the box Ask to save when I leave a changed patch in the Unsaved edits tray turns it back on.

Duplicating onto a slot, starting from the Initial Patch and reverting don't ask: Undo brings back what was there. The other question is before Open pack… or Load TracksGM would replace unsaved edits: it says how many, and offers to save them as a .syx bank first.

Playing from a MIDI keyboard or a Circuit Tracks

  1. Use Chrome, Edge or another browser with Web MIDI. Click Use MIDI keyboard at the top right and allow MIDI when the browser asks. PatchWorker then picks up any keyboard you plug in, and turns this on by itself on later visits.
  2. Notes play the patch you are editing, with their velocity. The sustain pedal works. Nothing is sent back to the keyboard.
  3. A Circuit Tracks works as a keyboard too. Play from its MIDI 1 track (channel 3) to edit and hear Synth 1, or its MIDI 2 track (channel 4) for Synth 2: these tracks send notes without playing the Circuit Tracks' own synths, so you only hear the browser. Playing from its Synth 1 or Synth 2 track also works, but then you hear the Circuit Tracks' synth as well; turn its volume down. MIDI 1 and MIDI 2 must be on their default channels (3 and 4), and MIDI note Tx on in its Setup view.
  4. Notes on any other channel play whichever synth is chosen with Synth 1 / Synth 2 in the patch bar.

The three pages

The pages and controls follow Novation Components' Synth editor.

MainOscillators 1 and 2 (wave, Index / pulse width, Interpolate, VSync, Cents, Semitones, Density, Detune, pitch-bend range), Mixer, Filter (type, Bypass routing, Frequency, Resonance, Q, Key Track, Env 2 > Freq, Drive and drive type), Envelopes 1 Amp / 2 Filter / 3 Mod, and LFOs 1 and 2.
EffectsChorus / Phaser, three-band Equaliser, Distortion, and Voice (Mono, Auto-Glide or Poly, Portamento, Pre-Glide, Keyboard Octave).
ModulationWhat the selected macro controls (up to four destinations, each with Start, End and Depth), and the 20-row mod matrix.

The eight Macros sit above every page. Their positions are saved in the patch. Click a macro to select it; the Modulation page then shows its destinations. The line under each knob names what it controls.

Values shown with ~ (Hz, ms, dB) are measured from a real Circuit Tracks, so "Frequency ~650 Hz" is what the hardware does at that setting. A Sync box swaps a rate knob to tempo-locked note lengths.

The TRACKS panel and playing projects

The TRACKS / EDITOR switch at the left of the patch bar swaps the editor's pages for a panel laid out like the Circuit Tracks: track buttons, view buttons, 32 pads and ▶. The patch bar, status line and macros stay. Views not built yet say so.

To hear your patches in a song, open a pack that has projects (TracksGM has none), go to Projects, press a project, then ▶ or Space. It loops until you stop it (▶ again, Space or Esc), and keeps playing if you switch to EDITOR. Synth 1 and Synth 2 play the pack's patches as you edit them, so every change is heard in the song. The editor opens the patch the chosen synth plays; choosing Synth 1 / Synth 2 (the patch bar, the track buttons, or a MIDI keyboard on channel 3 / 4) switches to that synth's patch. Choosing another patch, in the list or in Preset view, gives that synth the new patch to play, as the Circuit Tracks' Preset view does. Nothing is saved in the project: these are ways of listening for now.

The sound is the browser synth, the pack's own drum samples and the project's mixer, reverb, delay, drum settings and sidechain as measured on a real Circuit Tracks. MIDI 1 and MIDI 2, which play other gear from the Circuit Tracks, are sketched with a simple tone.

NoteTop two rows: the 16 steps of the pattern the track is playing (lit where a step plays something), with the play head. Synth 1 or 2: the bottom two rows are a keyboard that plays the patch, using the octave and velocity at the bottom of the page. Drum 1 to 4: the bottom-left pads play the drum's sample.
PresetSynth 1 or 2: the pack's 128 patches as four pages of 32 pads. The lit pad is the patch being edited; press a pad to edit that patch (it plays while you hold the pad, and the synth plays it in the project). An orange dot marks a changed patch, as in the patch list. Duplicate to pad… then a pad copies the patch being edited there and opens the copy for editing; Undo brings back what was there. Revert puts back the pack's version.
Drum 1 to 4: the pack's 64 samples as two pages. Press a pad to choose the drum's sample and hear it; loading a project sets each drum to the project's sample.
ProjectsThe pack's 64 project slots as two pages; saved projects are blue, the loaded one white.
PatternsOne column per track, patterns 1 to 4 down the rows (the second page has 5 to 8). Lit pads are the patterns each track plays, and a white ring shows the one playing now. Click a pattern to play it; Shift-click another to chain the patterns between them. This replaces the scene until you choose a scene again.
MixerTop row: mute each track. Bottom two rows: the 16 scenes (white: saved in the project; green: in the scene chain; pulsing: playing). Click a scene to play it; Shift-click another to chain the scenes between them. The sliders under the pads set each track's level (the Circuit Tracks' default is 100).
TempoTempo (40 to 240 BPM, also the Tempo box at the bottom), swing (20 to 80%) and Tap.

Editing a Circuit Tracks directly

Earlier test versions could link to a Circuit Tracks and edit what its synths were playing (Live, Get, Save to Tracks). That is switched off from v0.9 while PatchWorker's offline studio is built, because Novation Components already does it. To put your patches on the Circuit Tracks, download the pack (or .syx files) here and send them with Components.

Files

Open pack…Opens a .circuittrackspack from Components. Samples and projects are kept exactly as they are.
Download packSaves the pack with your patch edits and its name (up to 16 characters). If a project in the pack plays a patch you changed, PatchWorker offers to update that project's own copy of the patch too: projects carry their own copy of each synth patch, and only copies that still matched the pack are touched.
Open .syx…Loads one patch, or a bank of many, into the slots you choose. Patches from the original Circuit load too. You can also drop .syx and pack files anywhere on the page.
Download .syxThe current patch as a 350-byte SysEx file (also Save .syx beside the status tag).
Bank .syxAll 128 patches in one file.
Load TracksGMClears the session and starts again from the TracksGM pack (after a warning if you have unsaved edits).

How close is the browser sound?

The browser synth is an approximation, built on the Web Audio synth from circuit-tracks-tools by Namir Sayed-Ahmad Baraza, with its parameter curves replaced by CircuitGM's measurements of a real Circuit Tracks (filter cutoff and resonance, envelope times, velocity response, oscillator, noise and ring-modulation levels, LFO rate, modulation depth to pitch and filter, EQ, and Pre / Post FX level). It is good for shaping a sound; the hardware has the last word. Known differences:

  • The wavetable waves (Sine Table to Random Collection) are stand-in shapes. The Sine Table and the pulse and blend tables follow the measured behaviour; the vocal and random tables only resemble it.
  • When both oscillators play exactly the same pitch (same Semitones and Cents, no Density), the Circuit Tracks lets them sit at whatever point in their cycles earlier notes left them, so the same patch can come out louder or quieter, fuller or thinner, from one time to the next. The browser always uses the middle case.
  • At the top of the Resonance range the hardware's 24 dB filters and 12 dB band-pass ring by themselves; the browser stays just short of that.
  • Controls marked with a small orange ring (Q, pitch-bend range, LFO Slew) change the patch but not the browser sound. The same goes for Common Sync, and for mod matrix rows aimed at V-Sync, Pulse Width, Drive, LFO Rate and envelope Decay.
  • Keyboard Octave sets the octave the Circuit Tracks' own pads and patch preview start in. It does not shift notes played from this page or over MIDI, on the hardware or in the browser. Both play MIDI note 60 (middle C) at 261.6 Hz.
  • Distortion, filter drive, chorus and phaser are simpler than the hardware's.
  • How far a macro moves its destination is not yet measured on hardware, so macro ranges are estimates.

Every byte of a patch is kept, including anything PatchWorker does not show, so a patch saved here is exactly what you edited plus what was already there.

If something is not working

  • No sound in the browser: click a key once (browsers only start audio after a click), and check Volume.
  • A MIDI keyboard plays nothing: check that the top right says Keyboard: and its name. Safari has no Web MIDI. For a Circuit Tracks, switch on MIDI note Tx in its Setup view.
  • You hear the Circuit Tracks as well as the browser: play from its MIDI 1 or MIDI 2 track, or turn its volume down.
