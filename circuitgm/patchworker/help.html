PatchWorker help

PatchWorker edits the synth patches of a Novation Circuit Tracks, with no hardware needed: the page plays each patch itself. It opens with the TracksGM pack, plays the projects in a pack so you can hear patches in context, and what you make goes out as .syx patches or a whole .circuittrackspack to load with Novation Components. Nothing is uploaded: files stay on your computer.

Since v0.9 PatchWorker works offline only: the browser is the synth, and a MIDI keyboard (a Circuit Tracks too) just plays it. Editing a Circuit Tracks directly, as Components does, may come back later as a separate mode.

Quick start

  1. Pick a patch from the list on the left. The menu beside its search box chooses what the list shows: Patches, Changed patches, Samples (click one to hear it, click again to stop) or Projects (click one to load it, ▶ or a double-click to play it).
  2. Play it: click the keys along the bottom, type on the A row of your keyboard (W E T Y U are the black keys, Z / X change octave), or press Space to run a loop while you edit. Keys S / M / L in the bottom bar sets the keyboard's size.
  3. Turn knobs: drag up or down. Hold Shift for fine steps, scroll over a knob, or use the arrow keys. Double-click a knob to reset it. Click the number under a knob to type a value.
  4. Download pack saves the whole pack with your edits, ready to send to the Circuit Tracks with Novation Components. Patch ▾ → Download .syx saves just the current patch.

Projects play with their recorded knob movements (automation): drum pitch, decay, distortion and EQ, track levels, pans and FX sends, and the synths' macros, each held until the next recorded value. Macro movements turn the browser synth's macros only while the project plays; the patch itself is not changed.

Edits go straight into the pack in your browser, and your session is kept there, so closing the tab does not lose it. An orange dot marks every patch changed since the pack was opened. Undo (Ctrl/Cmd+Z) works per patch, Compare (hold it) plays the patch as the pack had it, and Patch ▾ → Revert puts it back (Undo brings your changes back again).

Saved, unsaved, and the Unsaved edits tray

The tag under the patch name says Saved or Edited · not saved. A patch is unsaved when you have changed it and not downloaded it since, as part of the pack (Download pack), a bank (Bank .syx) or its own .syx. While it is unsaved, Save .syx and Download pack sit beside the tag.

Every unsaved patch is listed under Unsaved edits on the left (the number is how many), newest first, with where it came from and when you changed it. Each has Open, .syx (download just that patch) and Discard (put it back as it was last saved; open it and press Undo to bring your edit back). Save all as .syx bank downloads them all in one file, which you can open here again or load with Components.

Saving is your decision. When you choose another patch while the one you are on has unsaved changes, PatchWorker asks once: Save .syx, Download pack, Not now (it stays in Unsaved edits) or Stay on this patch. It doesn't ask again about the same patch until you change it again. Tick Don't ask again to stop the question; the box Ask to save when I leave a changed patch in the Unsaved edits tray turns it back on.

Duplicating onto a slot, starting from the Initial Patch and reverting don't ask: Undo brings back what was there. The other question is before Open pack… or Load TracksGM would replace unsaved edits: it says how many, and offers to save them as a .syx bank first.

Playing from a MIDI keyboard or a Circuit Tracks

  1. Use Chrome, Edge or another browser with Web MIDI. Click Use MIDI keyboard at the top right and allow MIDI when the browser asks. PatchWorker then picks up any keyboard you plug in, and turns this on by itself on later visits.
  2. Notes play the patch you are editing, with their velocity. The sustain pedal works. Nothing is sent back to the keyboard.
  3. A Circuit Tracks works as a keyboard too. Play from its MIDI 1 track (channel 3) to edit and hear Synth 1, or its MIDI 2 track (channel 4) for Synth 2: these tracks send notes without playing the Circuit Tracks' own synths, so you only hear the browser. Playing from its Synth 1 or Synth 2 track also works, but then you hear the Circuit Tracks' synth as well; turn its volume down. MIDI 1 and MIDI 2 must be on their default channels (3 and 4), and MIDI note Tx on in its Setup view.
  4. Notes on any other channel play whichever synth is chosen with Synth 1 / Synth 2 in the patch bar.

The three pages

The pages and controls follow Novation Components' Synth editor.

MainOscillators 1 and 2 (wave, Index / pulse width, Interpolate, VSync, Cents, Semitones, Density, Detune, pitch-bend range), Mixer, Filter (type, Bypass routing, Frequency, Resonance, Q, Key Track, Env 2 > Freq, Drive and drive type), Envelopes 1 Amp / 2 Filter / 3 Mod, and LFOs 1 and 2.
EffectsChorus / Phaser, three-band Equaliser, Distortion, and Voice (Mono, Auto-Glide or Poly, Portamento, Pre-Glide, Keyboard Octave).
ModulationWhat the selected macro controls (up to four destinations, each with Start, End and Depth), and the 20-row mod matrix.

The eight Macros sit above every page. Their positions are saved in the patch. Click a macro to select it; the Modulation page then shows its destinations. The line under each knob names what it controls.

Values shown with ~ (Hz, ms, dB) are measured from a real Circuit Tracks, so "Frequency ~650 Hz" is what the hardware does at that setting. A Sync box swaps a rate knob to tempo-locked note lengths.

The TRACKS panel and playing projects

The TRACKS / EDITOR switch at the left of the patch bar swaps the editor's pages for a panel laid out like the Circuit Tracks' top panel (User Guide page 15): Master Volume, the eight macro knobs and Master Filter along the top; Scales, ▼ ▲, 1-16/17-32, Tempo/Swing, Clear, Duplicate, Save, Projects and Shift above the pads; Preset, the eight track buttons and Patterns; Note, Velocity, Gate and Pattern Settings down the left; Mixer, FX, Record and Play at the bottom right. The patch bar and status line stay; a strip under the pads names the view (the Circuit Tracks has no display). Buttons for parts not built yet are dimmed or say so.

Second legends work as on the hardware. A secondary view opens with Shift + the button, or by pressing the button again while in its view: Gate → Micro Step, Pattern Settings → Probability, FX → Side Chain (Note → Expand and Projects → Packs come later). Shift + Tempo/Swing taps the tempo. Shift is your computer's Shift key, or click Shift to arm it for the next button or pad. ▼ ▲ turn pages in Preset, Projects and Patterns views and change the octave in Note view. Master Filter filters the project playing (left low-pass, right high-pass, double-click for off); Master Volume is the page volume.

To hear your patches in a song, open a pack that has projects (TracksGM has none). The quickest way: choose a project under Project in the bottom bar (next to Loop) and press its ▶. In the TRACKS panel you can also go to Projects, press a project, then ▶ or Space. It loops until you stop it (▶ again, Space or Esc), and keeps playing if you switch between TRACKS and EDITOR. Once a project is loaded, Space plays and stops it instead of the editor's loop (the Loop ▶ still works). Synth 1 and Synth 2 play the pack's patches as you edit them, so every change is heard in the song. The editor opens the patch the chosen synth plays; choosing Synth 1 / Synth 2 (the patch bar, the track buttons, or a MIDI keyboard on channel 3 / 4) switches to that synth's patch. Choosing another patch, in the list or in Preset view, gives that synth the new patch to play, as the Circuit Tracks' Preset view does.

Editing steps and saving projects

The step views work as in the Circuit Tracks User Guide (pages 38 to 55 and 75). The top two rows are the steps of the pattern the track plays (while playing, the pattern under the play head); a number on each pad gives the step. Edits are heard straight away. The status strip says edited, not saved until you save, and Revert project under the pads drops the edits.

  • Note, Synth 1 / 2 and MIDI 1 / 2: press a step to choose it (it turns red, and when stopped plays its notes). The keys of its notes turn red with a white dot; press a key to add that note to the step, or press a red key to remove it (up to 6 notes a step). Press the step again, or Esc, to play the keys normally. On the hardware you hold the step pad while pressing keys; a mouse can't hold one pad and click another, so here one click chooses the step (holding works too on a touch screen). A new note takes the velocity set at the bottom of the page (96 with Fixed Velocity), and the gate and micro step of notes already on the step. The keys follow the project's scale (see Scales below).
  • Note, Drum 1 to 4 (User Guide pages 24 and 60 to 62): the bottom two rows are 16 of the pack's 64 samples, in the order of Preset view; ▼ ▲ show the other three pages. The track's active sample is bright. Tap a sample pad to make it the track's sample (you hear it, and the project plays it on every hit without a sample of its own); hold it to only hear it. Tap a step to add a hit with the track's sample, tap it again to remove it. A step can have its own sample (a sample flip, shown pink): hold a sample pad until it turns red, then press steps to give them that sample (press the sample pad again or Esc to finish); or hold a step until it turns red, then tap the sample pad it should play. Holding a step lights its sample red on the sample pads, turning to its page, as on the hardware. The pads show slot numbers only; hover a sample pad or a step to read the sample's name in the status strip (Preset view lists the names). A mouse can't hold one pad while clicking another, so a long press arms the pad instead and it stays armed.
  • Velocity: press a step, then one of the 16 pads below: pad 1 is 8, pad 12 is 96, pad 16 is 127. With notes of different velocities on a step, bright pads show the lowest and dim pads the highest. Shift + Velocity turns Fixed Velocity on or off.
  • Gate (synth and MIDI tracks): press a step, then the pad for how many steps its notes sound. Press the highest lit pad again to shorten the gate by a sixth of a step; after five presses it is whole again.
  • Micro Step (User Guide pp. 48-51, 66): each step is six micro steps. On a drum track Gate view is its Micro Step view: press a step, then pads 17-22 turn its micro steps 1-6 on or off (all six lit dimly, the ones that play brightly). Several can be on, and each one plays the hit, all at the step's velocity, sample and probability. A micro step on an empty step adds a hit there. On a synth or MIDI track press Gate again for Micro Step view. Pads 25-30 are the step's notes, in the order they were added (all chosen to start with; click one to choose only it, Shift-click to choose several, and each plays when pressed). Pads 17-22 set when the chosen notes start (1 on the step, 2-6 one to five sixths late), and pad 24 ties the step forward (set the gate long enough to reach the next note).
  • Probability (Shift + Pattern Settings, or press it again): press a step, then one of the 8 pads on row 3: 8 pads 100%, 4 pads 50%, 1 pad 12.5%.
  • Pattern Settings: press a step to make it the pattern's last step (sand). Hold Shift (or click Shift first) to see and move the first step. Row 3 is the sync rate (1/4 to 1/32T; 1/16 is the default), pads 29 to 32 the play order (forward, reverse, ping-pong, random). Steps outside the pattern are dark, or dim red if something is stored there.
  • Clear: click it to turn it on (red), then press steps to clear them (their notes or hit go and their probability returns to 100%). Click Clear again or Esc to turn it off. With Clear on, 1-16/17-32 puts the pattern back to 16 steps (steps 17 to 32 are kept, unheard).
  • Duplicate: click it to turn it on, press the step to copy, then each step to copy it to. With Duplicate on, 1-16/17-32 copies steps 1 to 16 (and their automation) to 17 to 32.
  • 1-16/17-32: a pattern of 16 steps becomes 32 steps; after that it shows steps 1 to 16 (blue) or 17 to 32 (orange).

Scales (User Guide pages 30 to 32): rows 1 and 2 choose the root note, laid out as one octave of a piano (bright pads are the notes in the scale, the root is sand); rows 3 and 4 choose one of the 16 scales. Note view's keyboard follows them: with Chromatic it is a one-octave piano; with any other scale each of the two rows holds eight notes of the scale rising from the root, and the top row starts on the last note of the bottom row (the root pads are paler). The scale and root belong to the project and are saved with it. Changing the root transposes the project's synth and MIDI notes. Changing the scale is not destructive: a note that is not in the new scale plays the nearest note in it, and choosing the old scale again plays it as before. (Which way a note exactly halfway goes is not in the guide: PatchWorker goes down, to be checked on a Circuit Tracks.) With no project loaded, Scales only sets the keyboard, which starts as Chromatic.

Piano roll and drum grid (not on the Circuit Tracks, which has no screen): in Note view, the Piano roll button under the pads (Drum grid on a drum track) shows the pattern instead of the pads; Pads (or Note) goes back. In Patterns view, double-click a pattern to open it there; press Patterns to go back. They edit the same pattern as the pads, and Save keeps the changes.

  • Piano roll (Synth 1 / 2, MIDI 1 / 2): one row per note (only the scale's notes unless the scale is Chromatic), one column per step (32 for a longer pattern; steps outside the pattern are shaded). Click an empty cell to add a note, dragging right to make it longer; drag a note to move it; drag its right edge to change its length (whole steps, or sixths of a step with Shift held); click a note to remove it; scroll the mouse wheel over a note to change its velocity (shown by its brightness). Click a key on the left to hear it. ▼ ▲ scroll an octave. A step holds up to 6 notes.
  • Drum grid: Drum 1 to 4 in rows, each the pattern its track plays (shown on the left with the track's sample). Click a cell to add a hit with the track's sample, or remove one; scroll the wheel over a hit for its velocity. Pink hits play a sample of their own, with its number on the cell; hover for the name. Change samples in Note view.

Expanded Note view: press Note again (or Shift + Note) for a four-row keyboard, two more octaves of the scale (with Chromatic, two octaves of piano). Press Note again to go back.

Save as on the hardware (User Guide page 60): press Save once (it flashes), then again to save over the project loaded. To save to another slot, go to Projects, press Save, then the pad of the slot (the original keeps the version saved before). A project is saved with its steps and pattern settings, its scale and root, the tempo and swing, and the patch each synth plays (its slot and the project's own copy of it). Save puts the project into the browser's copy of the pack: it is kept between visits, but nothing is written to disk. Your pack file stays as it was until you use Download pack, which writes a new file. In Projects view a dot marks a project with unsaved edits, an orange ring one saved here. Mixer levels and mutes, the patterns chosen in Patterns view, and the scene chain are not saved yet.

Project copies. A project keeps its own copy of each synth's patch, which can differ from the pack's patch in the same slot. When it really differs (not just in the macro knob positions), the song plays the copy, so the editor edits the copy while that project is loaded: Project copy shows next to the patch name. Changes to it are project edits, with their own undo: Save on the TRACKS panel keeps them, and the pack's patch is not touched. Save to pack slot… (under the patch name, or in the Patch menu) puts the copy into a pack slot, the project's own slot unless you choose another; the project then plays that pack patch (save the project to keep it). Edit the pack's patch shows the slot's own patch instead, while the song goes on playing the copy; Edit the project copy goes back.

The sound is the browser synth, the pack's own drum samples and the project's mixer, reverb, delay, drum settings and sidechain as measured on a real Circuit Tracks. MIDI 1 and MIDI 2, which play other gear from the Circuit Tracks, are sketched with a simple tone.

NoteTop two rows: the 16 steps of the pattern the track is playing (lit where a step plays something), with the play head; see Editing steps. Synth 1 or 2: the bottom two rows are a keyboard that plays the patch, using the octave and velocity at the bottom of the page. Drum 1 to 4: the bottom-left pads play the drum's sample.

The lit pad is the patch being edited; press a pad to edit that patch (it plays while you hold the pad, and the synth plays it in the project). An orange dot marks a changed patch, as in the patch list. Duplicate to pad… then a pad copies the patch being edited there and opens the copy for editing; Undo brings back what was there. Revert puts back the pack's version.
Drum 1 to 4: the pack's 64 samples as two pages. Press a pad to choose the drum's sample and hear it; loading a project sets each drum to the project's sample.
ProjectsThe pack's 64 project slots as two pages; saved projects are blue, the loaded one white. A dot marks unsaved edits, an orange ring a project saved here. After Save, every pad flashes: press one to save there.

Click a pattern to play it; hold it and press another (or Shift-click) to chain the patterns between them. This replaces the scene until you choose a scene again.
MixerTop row: mute each track. Bottom two rows: the 16 scenes (white: saved in the project; green: in the scene chain; pulsing: playing). Click a scene to play it; hold it and press another (or Shift-click) to chain the scenes between them. The sliders under the pads set each track's level (the Circuit Tracks' default is 100).
TempoTempo (40 to 240 BPM, also the Tempo box at the bottom), swing (20 to 80%) and Tap.

Editing a Circuit Tracks directly

Earlier test versions could link to a Circuit Tracks and edit what its synths were playing (Live, Get, Save to Tracks). That is switched off from v0.9 while PatchWorker's offline studio is built, because Novation Components already does it. To put your patches on the Circuit Tracks, download the pack (or .syx files) here and send them with Components.

Files

Open pack…Opens a .circuittrackspack from Components. Samples and projects are kept exactly as they are.
Download packWrites a new pack file with your patch edits and the projects saved on the TRACKS panel. It asks for the pack's name (up to 16 characters, the name Components shows), suggesting a new one such as "Blue Monday 2" so the pack you opened is never confused with the edited one; the file goes to your Downloads folder under that name (the browser adds a number rather than replace a file already there). If a project in the pack plays a patch you changed, PatchWorker offers to update that project's own copy of the patch too: projects carry their own copy of each synth patch, and only copies that still matched the pack are touched.
Open .syx…Loads one patch, or a bank of many, into the slots you choose. Patches from the original Circuit load too. You can also drop .syx and pack files anywhere on the page.
Download .syxThe current patch as a 350-byte SysEx file (also Save .syx beside the status tag).
Bank .syxAll 128 patches in one file.
Load TracksGMClears the session and starts again from the TracksGM pack (after a warning if you have unsaved edits).

How close is the browser sound?

The browser synth is an approximation, built on the Web Audio synth from circuit-tracks-tools by Namir Sayed-Ahmad Baraza, with its parameter curves replaced by CircuitGM's measurements of a real Circuit Tracks (filter cutoff and resonance, envelope times, velocity response, oscillator, noise and ring-modulation levels, LFO rate, modulation depth to pitch and filter, EQ, and Pre / Post FX level). It is good for shaping a sound; the hardware has the last word. Known differences:

  • The wavetable waves (Sine Table to Random Collection) are stand-in shapes. The Sine Table and the pulse and blend tables follow the measured behaviour; the vocal and random tables only resemble it.
  • When both oscillators play exactly the same pitch (same Semitones and Cents, no Density), the Circuit Tracks lets them sit at whatever point in their cycles earlier notes left them, so the same patch can come out louder or quieter, fuller or thinner, from one time to the next. The browser always uses the middle case.
  • At the top of the Resonance range the hardware's 24 dB filters and 12 dB band-pass ring by themselves; the browser stays just short of that.
  • Controls marked with a small orange ring (Q, pitch-bend range, LFO Slew) change the patch but not the browser sound. The same goes for Common Sync, and for mod matrix rows aimed at V-Sync, Pulse Width, Drive, LFO Rate and envelope Decay.
  • Keyboard Octave sets the octave the Circuit Tracks' own pads and patch preview start in. It does not shift notes played from this page or over MIDI, on the hardware or in the browser. Both play MIDI note 60 (middle C) at 261.6 Hz.
  • Distortion, filter drive, chorus and phaser are simpler than the hardware's.
  • How far a macro moves its destination is not yet measured on hardware, so macro ranges are estimates.

Every byte of a patch is kept, including anything PatchWorker does not show, so a patch saved here is exactly what you edited plus what was already there.

If something is not working

  • No sound in the browser: click a key once (browsers only start audio after a click), and check Volume.
  • A MIDI keyboard plays nothing: check that the top right says Keyboard: and its name. Safari has no Web MIDI. For a Circuit Tracks, switch on MIDI note Tx in its Setup view.
  • You hear the Circuit Tracks as well as the browser: play from its MIDI 1 or MIDI 2 track, or turn its volume down.
